\subsection{Field extensions}

Most fields I know have obvious prime subfields, the set of elements formed by applying all possible field operations to $1$. In $\bR$, $\bC$, and algebraic number fields this is $\bQ$, in finite fields this $\bF_p$. If $k$ is the prime field of $K$, there is obviously only one embeddings $k\subset K$, and for any other field $k\subset L$, all of the homomorphisms $f:K\rightarrow L$ will fix $k$. So it is equivalent to consider the category of fields with prime subfield $k$, and the category of $k$-extensions.   

\subsection{Normal extensions}

\begin{definition}
    An extension $k\subset K$ is \textbf{normal} if, for all $k\subset \Omega$, the \textit{right} action \[
        \Emb_k(K,\Omega) \curvearrowleft \Aut_k(K)
    \] by precompostion, is transitive.
\end{definition}

This is equivalent to saying, if we have two embeddings $x_1,x_2:L\hookrightarrow\Omega$, then there exists some automorphism $\sigma\in\Aut(L/K)$ such that $x_2 = x_1\sigma$. If we just know $x_2(L)\subset x_1(L)$ as sets, this is also a field extension, and as the two fields are isomorphic they must in fact be equal in $\Omega$. This embedding is exactly the required $\sigma$.

\begin{lemma}
    Suppose $k\subset K$ is normal, and $k\subset F \subset K\subset \Omega$ is a tower, then the natural restriction: \[
    \Emb_k(K,\Omega)\hookrightarrow \Emb_k(F,\Omega)
    \] is surjective.
    \begin{proof}
        For any $x\in\Emb_k(F,\Omega)$, we can form the diagram: \[
        \begin{tikzcd}
            K \arrow[r, phantom, sloped, "\subset"] \arrow[rd, dashed] & \Omega'\\
            F \arrow[u, phantom, sloped, "\subset"] \arrow[r, hookrightarrow, "x", swap] & \Omega \arrow[u, phantom, sloped, "\subset"] \\
            k \arrow[u, phantom, sloped, "\subset"]
        \end{tikzcd} 
        \] where $\Omega'$ is produced by (A4), and as $k\subset K$ is normal, the inclusion $K\subset\Omega'$ factors through $K\subset\Omega$.
    \end{proof}
\end{lemma}

So when $k\subset K$ is normal, the left action $\Aut(K/k)\curvearrowright\Emb_k(F,K)$ by post-composition is transitive. Specifically, if $f\in k[X]$ is irreducible and splits completely in $K$, then $\Aut(K/k)$ acts transitively on these roots, just by taking $F=k(a)$ for $a$ some root in $K$.

\begin{lemma}
    The natrual composition with the given $K\subset\Omega$: \[
        \Emb_k(F,K)\rightarrow \Emb_k(F,\Omega)
    \] is also surjective.
    \begin{proof}
        By the previous lemma it suffices to consider $\sigma\in \Emb_k(K,\Omega)$ restricted to $F$, but we already have a canonical $K\subset\Omega$, so $\sigma$ is just represented by some element of $\Aut(K/k)$ which we just restrict to $F$ to produce our desired $\Emb_k(F,K)$.
    \end{proof}
\end{lemma}

This says any $k$-embedding $x:F\rightarrow \Omega$ still lands $F$ in $K$, this is a stronger statement of the definition of $K$ being a normal extensions, in fact if we take $F=K$ we recover this.

\begin{theorem}[Characterisation of normal extensions]
    For an extension $k\subset K$, tfae:\begin{itemize}
        \item[(1)] for all irreducible $f\in k[X]$, either $f$ has no roots in $K$ or $f$ splits completely in $K$;
        \item[(2)] there exists some $f\in k[X]$ (not necessarily irreducible) such that $K$ is the splitting field of $f$ over $k$;
        \item[(3)] $k\subset K$ is a normal extension.
    \end{itemize}
    \begin{proof}
        $(1)\implies(2)$ Write $K=k(a_1,\ldots,a_n)$ and let $f_i$ be the minimal polynomial of $a_i$ over $k$, then $K$ is the splitting field of $f=f_1\cdots f_n$ over $k$.

        $(2)\implies(3)$ For all $K\subset \Omega$ and $x\in\Emb_k(K,\Omega)$, we just have to show $x(K)\subset K$. If $a$ is a root of $f$ in $K$, then so is $x(a)$ which must also be in $K$, as $K$ is generated by these roots, we are done.

        $(3)\implies(1)$ If $f$ has any root $a\in K$, and we let $\Omega$ be the splitting field of $f$ in $K$, we can apply the previous lemma to the tower\[k\subset k(a)\subset K \subset \Omega\]
        yielding the series of isomorphisms:\[
    \{\text{roots of $f$ in $\Omega$}\} \cong \Emb_k(k(a),\Omega) \cong \Emb_k(k(a),K) \cong \{\text{roots of $f$ in $K$}\}\qedhere\]
    \end{proof}
\end{theorem}
